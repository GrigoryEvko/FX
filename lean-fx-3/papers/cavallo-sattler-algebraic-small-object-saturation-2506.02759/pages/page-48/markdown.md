### 4.3 Extension operations

As a second application, we consider the construction of *extension operations*. The general pattern is that we have some notion of structure on objects of our category and want to show that any structure on the domain of a left map induces a compatible structure on its codomain. The motivating example is the extension of *fibrations along trivial cofibrations*, where the structure on an object is a fibration over it:

**Example 4.3.1.** A Quillen premodel category [Bar19] has the *fibration extension property* when for any trivial cofibration $m: A \rightleftarrows B$ and fibration $p: X \rightarrow A$ over its domain, there is a square

$$\begin{array}{c} X \longrightarrow Y \\ p \downarrow \quad \downarrow \\ A \longmapsto \xrightarrow[m]{} B \end{array}$$

exhibiting $p$ as a pullback of a fibration over $B$.

The property is used in homotopical semantics of type theory, for example by Cisinski [Cis14, Proposition 2.21] to build interpretations of type theory from Quillen model categories, and conversely by Sattler [Sat17] and others [Awo23; CS25; ACCRS24] to build Quillen model categories from or in conjunction with interpretations of type theory. It is, in particular, connected to fibrancy of universes classifying fibrations: under some conditions, a fibration $p: \tilde{U} \rightarrow U$ will have a fibrant base object $U$ if and only if $U$-small fibrations extend along trivial cofibrations [Sat17, Remark 7.6] [Ste19, Proposition 2.2.4]. It and related properties also appear in homotopy theory more broadly (*e.g.*, [JT99, Lemma 1.7.1] [Cis19, Corollary 5.2.11]).

In these situations, one often first shows explicitly that fibrations extend along *generating* trivial cofibrations. When the extension property can be reformulated as fibrancy of a hierarchy of universes, extension along generators immediately gives extension along all trivial cofibrations. If one is primarily interested in building a model category, however, it would be conceptually cleaner not to depend on a stratification of the category by such a hierarchy.

Instead, one can use a decomposition of trivial cofibrations by some kind of small object argument, as is done in Sattler [Sat17, Corollary 7.4] and Gambino et al. [GSS22, Corollary 4.2.7]. We show in this section how to carry out such an argument for a class of trivial cofibrations generated using the *algebraic* small object argument, where previous work was limited to factorization systems generated by Quillen's argument.

**Remark 4.3.2.** An alternative approach to the following result is described in unpublished work of the second author [Sat25], using the fine-grained functoriality of Section 2 directly in place of this article's saturation machinery. That approach has the advantage of avoiding pushforward manipulations by working with individual extension problems in place of extension operations.

We consider notions of structure on objects of a category given as a Grothendieck fibration over that category. We first fix some notation.

**Notation 4.3.3.** Let $P: \mathcal{E} \rightarrow \mathcal{B}$ be a Grothendieck fibration.

- For $a \in \mathcal{B}$, we write $\mathcal{E}_a$ for the fiber of $P$ over $a$.
- Similarly, for $u: a \rightarrow b$ in $\mathcal{B}$ we write $\mathcal{E}_u^\rightarrow$ for the fiber of $P^\rightarrow: \mathcal{E}^\rightarrow \rightarrow \mathcal{B}^\rightarrow$ over $u$.
- We assume that our fibrations come with a choice of cartesian lifts (*i.e.*, are *cloven*) and write $\bar{u}e: u^*e \rightarrow e$ for the chosen cartesian lift of some $e \in \mathcal{E}_a$ along $u: b \rightarrow a$.
- We write $P_{\mathcal{B}\text{-cart}}: \mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}\text{-cart}} \rightarrow \mathcal{B}$ for the restriction of $P$ to the wide subcategory $\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}\text{-cart}}$ consisting of $P$-cartesian morphisms (sometimes called the *fibration of objects*).
