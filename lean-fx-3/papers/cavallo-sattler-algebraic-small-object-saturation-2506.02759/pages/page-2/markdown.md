# 1 Introduction

Quillen's *small object argument* [Qui67] builds, under some conditions, a weak factorization system (WFS) in a category $\mathcal{E}$ from a generating set of left maps in $\mathcal{E}$. Given such a set $S \subseteq \mathcal{E}^\rightarrow$, the class $\mathcal{R} := S^\oplus$ of maps right lifting against $S$ and the class $\mathcal{L} := {}^\oplus\mathcal{R}$ of maps left lifting against $\mathcal{R}$ constitute a WFS $(\mathcal{L}, \mathcal{R})$ exactly if every map factors as one in $\mathcal{L}$ followed by one in $\mathcal{R}$, in which case one says $(\mathcal{L}, \mathcal{R})$ is *cofibrantly generated by $S$*. The small object argument constructs these factorizations.

Garner's *algebraic small object argument* [Gar09] is a refinement of Quillen's argument that generalizes it along two axes: it takes a diagram of generators $u: \mathcal{J} \rightarrow \mathcal{E}^\rightarrow$ as input—as opposed to a set, which is the special case where $\mathcal{J}$ is discrete—and produces an *algebraic weak factorization system* (AWFS) as output. A WFS is *generated by a diagram* $u: \mathcal{J} \rightarrow \mathcal{E}^\rightarrow$ if its right maps are those $f$ equipped with a choice of filler for each lifting problem $\alpha: u_i \rightarrow f$ such that for every $t: j \rightarrow i$ in $\mathcal{J}$ and $\alpha: u_i \rightarrow f$, the triangle

$$\begin{array}{c} A_j \longrightarrow A_i \longrightarrow X \\ \downarrow \quad u_t \quad \downarrow \quad \alpha \quad \uparrow \\ B_j \longrightarrow B_i \longrightarrow Y \end{array} \quad (1.1)$$

formed by the solutions for $\alpha: u_i \rightarrow f$ and $\alpha u_t: u_j \rightarrow f$ commutes. This generalization captures more WFS's of interest [Rie11, §4.3] [Ros17, Example 2.6], in particular WFS's used in constructive interpretations of homotopy type theory [Swa16; GS17; CMS20; Awo23; ACCRS24; CS25]. With an *algebraic* WFS, one has *categories* of left and right maps with useful properties such as closure under small colimits and limits respectively [GT06].

The left factor of a factorization produced by Quillen's argument is, by construction, a *cell complex*: a transfinite composite of cobase changes (*i.e.*, pushouts along arbitrary maps) of small coproducts of generating maps. Defining a *cellular class of maps* to be one closed under transfinite composition, cobase change, small coproducts, it follows that any cellular class containing the generators contains the left factor of every chosen factorization. Furthermore, any left map can be written as a codomain retract of the left factor of a factorization [Hov99, Lemma 1.1.9]. Thus the class of left maps has a useful universal property: defining a *saturated class of maps* to be a cellular class closed under codomain retracts, the class of left maps is the least saturated class containing the generators.

The algebraic small object argument does not come with an obvious equivalent for this story. Even if we start from a discrete diagram of generators, it is not clear how to extract a cell complex decomposition from Garner's construction [Gar07, §6.4]. For a non-discrete diagram, it is not even clear what the notion of cell complex should be.

We contribute an analogue to the concept of saturated class of maps for AWFS's and show that the category of left maps of a cofibrantly generated AWFS is 'least' among the 'saturated classes' containing the diagram that generates it.
