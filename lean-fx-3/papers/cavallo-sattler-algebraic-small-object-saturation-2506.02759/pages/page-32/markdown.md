### 3.4 Structured points on free monads

We want to extend an assignment of $\mathbb{A}$-structures from the generators of a cofibrantly generated AWFS $(\mathsf{L},\mathsf{R})$ to the left factor $Lf$ of every map $f$. In the algebraic small object argument, $L$ arises indirectly as the domain component of the unit $\eta$ of the monad $\mathsf{R}$. We abstract away the details of this case and consider the problem of extending an assignment of $\mathbb{A}$-structures from the unit of a pointed endofunctor to the unit of its free monad.

We define “$\mathbb{A}$-structures on a unit” in a somewhat indirect way that will be easiest to manipulate in our proofs; we unpack the intuition in Remark 3.4.3 below.

Notation 3.4.1. Let $\mathcal{A} \xrightarrow{F} \mathcal{C} \xleftarrow{G} \mathcal{B}$ be a cospan of categories. Let $\mathsf{T} = (T, \tau)$ be a pointed endofunctor on $\mathcal{A}$ such that $F$ defines a strict morphism of pointed endofunctors $(\mathcal{A}, \mathsf{T}) \to (\mathcal{C}, \mathsf{Id}_{\mathcal{C}})$. We write $\mathsf{T} \times_{\mathcal{C}} \mathcal{B}$ for the pointed endofunctor on $\mathcal{A} \times_{\mathcal{C}} \mathcal{B}$ sending a pair $(A, B) \in \mathcal{A} \times_{\mathcal{C}} \mathcal{B}$ to $(TA, B)$, with evident unit. When $\mathsf{M}$ is a monad and $F$ defines a strict morphism of monads $(\mathcal{A}, \mathsf{M}) \to (\mathcal{C}, \mathsf{Id}_{\mathcal{C}})$, we write $\mathsf{M} \times_{\mathcal{C}} \mathcal{B}$ for the analogous monad.

Definition 3.4.2. Let $\mathsf{T}$ be a pointed endofunctor on $\mathcal{E}$ and $U: \mathbb{A} \to \operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{E})$ be a notion of composable structure. An $\mathbb{A}$-point $(\overline{\mathsf{T}}, \theta)$ over $\mathsf{T}$ consists of a pointed endofunctor $\overline{\mathsf{T}}$ on $\mathbb{A}^{\natural}$ for which $\operatorname{dom}_{\natural}: (\mathbb{A}^{\natural}, \overline{\mathsf{T}}) \to (\mathcal{E}, \operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{E}})$ and $\operatorname{cod}_{\natural}: (\mathbb{A}^{\natural}, \overline{\mathsf{T}}) \to (\mathcal{E}, \mathsf{T})$ are strict morphisms of pointed endofunctors, together with a natural isomorphism $\theta$ making $(\star, \theta): (\mathbb{A}^{\natural} \times_{\mathcal{E}} \mathbb{A}^{\natural}, \overline{\mathsf{T}} \times_{\mathcal{E}} \mathbb{A}^{\natural}) \to (\mathbb{A}^{\natural}, \overline{\mathsf{T}})$ a strong morphism of pointed endofunctors.

If $\gamma: \mathsf{T} \to \mathsf{T}'$ is a morphism of pointed endofunctors on $\mathcal{E}$ and $(\overline{\mathsf{T}}, \theta)$ and $(\overline{\mathsf{T}}', \theta')$ are $\mathbb{A}$-points over $\mathsf{T}$ and $\mathsf{T}'$ respectively, then a morphism of $\mathbb{A}$-points $(\overline{\mathsf{T}}, \theta) \to (\overline{\mathsf{T}}', \theta')$ over $\gamma$ is a morphism of pointed endofunctors $\overline{\gamma}: \overline{\mathsf{T}} \to \overline{\mathsf{T}}'$ such that $\operatorname{dom}_{\natural} \overline{\gamma} = \operatorname{id}$, $\operatorname{cod}_{\natural} \overline{\gamma} = \gamma$, and

$$\begin{array}{ccc} \overline{T}(\pi_0-) \star (\pi_1-) & \xrightarrow{\overline{\gamma}(\pi_0-) \star (\pi_1-)} & \overline{T}'(\pi_0-) \star (\pi_1-) \\ \theta \downarrow & & \downarrow \theta' \\ \overline{T}(\pi_0-\star \pi_1-) & \xrightarrow{\overline{\gamma}(\pi_0-\star \pi_1-)} & \overline{T}'(\pi_0-\star \pi_1-) \end{array}$$

commutes.

Mutatis mutandis, we have definitions of $\mathbb{A}$-point $(\overline{\mathsf{M}}, \theta)$ over a monad $\mathsf{M}$ and morphism of $\mathbb{A}$-points $\overline{\gamma}: (\overline{\mathsf{M}}, \theta) \to (\overline{\mathsf{M}}', \theta')$ over a morphism of monads $\gamma: \mathsf{M} \to \mathsf{M}'$.

Remark 3.4.3. Let $(T, \tau)$ be a pointed endofunctor and $((\overline{T}, \overline{\tau}), \theta)$ be an $\mathbb{A}$-point over $(T, \tau)$. Since $\theta$ tells us that $\overline{T}(\boldsymbol{f}) \cong \overline{T}(\mathbf{id}_B \star \boldsymbol{f}) \cong \overline{T}(\mathbf{id}_B) \star \boldsymbol{f}$ for $\boldsymbol{f}: A \leftrightarrow B$, we see that $\overline{T}$ is defined up to isomorphism by its restriction to vertical identities. For a given $A \in \mathcal{E}$, $\overline{\tau}_{\mathbf{id}_A}$ is a square of the form

$$\begin{array}{c} A \xlongequal{\quad} A \\ \mathbf{id}_A \downarrow \qquad \qquad \qquad \downarrow \overline{T}(\mathbf{id}_A) \\ A \xrightarrow{\tau_A} TA. \end{array}$$

When $U$ is left-connected, there is only one such square of this form, namely the left connection for $\overline{T}(\mathbf{id}_A)$. Conversely, again when $U$ is left-connected, any functor $\boldsymbol{\tau}: \mathcal{E} \to \mathbb{A}^{\natural}$ such that $U^{\natural} \boldsymbol{\tau} = \tau$ determines an $\mathbb{A}$-point $((\boldsymbol{\tau}_1, \Delta_1), \alpha)$ over $\mathsf{T}$ where $\boldsymbol{\tau}_1(\boldsymbol{f}) = \boldsymbol{\tau}_B \star \boldsymbol{f}$, $\Delta_1$ sends $\boldsymbol{f}$ to the square

$$\begin{array}{c} A \xlongequal{\quad} A \xlongequal{\quad} A \\ \downarrow \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \\ \boldsymbol{f} \downarrow \qquad \rho \qquad B \xlongequal{\quad} B \xlongequal{\quad} B \\ \downarrow \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \\ B \xlongequal{\quad} B \xrightarrow{\tau_B} TB \end{array}$$
