(b) $I_!$ preserves levelwise complemented morphisms and $\delta^0, \delta^1$ are valued in levelwise complemented monomorphisms.

Example 4.1.6. Consider presheaves on the category $\mathcal{C} = \mathbf{FinSet}_+$ of inhabited finite sets, which are called symmetric simplicial sets (see, e.g., Grandis [Gra01]). The category $\mathcal{C}$ is the idempotent completion of the full subcategory $\square_{\mathrm{B}} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{C}$ of objects of the form $\{0,1\}^n$ for $n \geq 0$ [Cam23, Proposition 8.11], which is known as the Boolean cube category [BM17]; thus $\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C}) \simeq \mathrm{PSh}(\square_{\mathrm{B}})$ can also be called the category of Boolean cubical sets.

The functor $(-\cdot) \times \{0,1\} \colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}$ defines a functorial cylinder whose left Kan extension $(-\cdot) \times \mathbb{I} \colon \mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C})$ is product with the representable $\mathbb{I} = \mathcal{X}\{0,1\} \in \mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C})$. For a cofibration classifier, we can take the levelwise complemented subobject classifier $\top_{\mathrm{lc}} \colon 1 \mapsto \Omega_{\mathrm{lc}}$, with $(\Omega_{\mathrm{lc}})_I$ defined to be the set of sieves on $I \in \mathcal{C}$ with decidable membership and $\top_{\mathrm{lc}}$ to select the total sieve. In a classical metatheory, $\Omega$ is simply the subobject classifier. Every morphism in $\mathcal{C}$ factors as a split epimorphism followed by a monomorphism, so every decidable sieve on $I \in \mathcal{C}$ is generated by a (necessarily) finite collection of monomorphisms, meaning that every levelwise complemented subobject is a finite colimit of representables.$^4$ Thus $1 \mapsto \Omega_{\mathrm{lc}}$ is locally finite, and we have a finitary uniform fibration configuration $(\top_{\mathrm{lc}}, (-\cdot) \times \{0,1\})$.

The uniform fibration WFS on this configuration is the (trivial cofibration, fibration) WFS of a Quillen model structure whose cofibrations are those maps classified by $1 \mapsto \Omega_{\mathrm{lc}}$ [GS17; Sat17], and $\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C})$ admits a constructive interpretation of homotopy type theory in which types are the uniform fibrations [CCHM15; OP18; LOPS18].

This example is one of many where $\mathrm{PSh}(\mathcal{C})$ is a category of cubical sets, that is, of presheaves over a monoidal category whose objects are powers of an interval object (see, e.g., Grandis and Mauri [GM03] or Buchholtz and Morehouse [BM17]).

Example 4.1.7. Cohen et al. [CCHM15] give a model of homotopy type theory in De Morgan cubical sets, presheaves over the Lawvere theory $\square_{\mathrm{DM}}$ of De Morgan algebras. Product with the generating object of the Lawvere theory defines a functorial cylinder; as in boolean cubical sets, its left Kan extension is product $(-\cdot) \times \mathbb{I}$ with an interval object $\mathbb{I} \in \mathrm{PSh}(\square_{\mathrm{DM}})$. Cohen et al. interpret types as the uniform fibrations for a cofibration classifier they call the face lattice [CCHM15, §4.1], which is the minimal subclassifier $\mathbb{F} \subseteq \Omega_{\mathrm{lc}}$ classifying the face inclusions $\mathbb{I}^k \times \delta^i \times \mathbb{I}^{n-k} \colon \mathbb{I}^n \mapsto \mathbb{I}^{n+1}$ and closed under finite intersection and union. The face lattice excludes for example the diagonal subobject $\Delta_{\mathbb{I}} \colon \mathbb{I} \mapsto \mathbb{I} \times \mathbb{I}$. It is locally finite essentially by construction.

This example is notable in our context because it is not generated by any set, even in a classical metatheory. Suppose for sake of contradiction that we had a generating set $S \subseteq \mathrm{PSh}(\square_{\mathrm{DM}})^-$ of trivial cofibrations, so that every trivial cofibration is a cell complex of maps in $S$. For any cardinal $\lambda$, we can attach $\lambda$ copies of the 2-cube along their diagonals to obtain an object

$$\begin{array}{c} \coprod_{\lambda} \mathbb{I} \xrightarrow{\nabla} \mathbb{I} \\ \coprod_{\lambda} \Delta_{\mathbb{I}} \Big\downarrow \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \qquad \\ \coprod_{\lambda} (\mathbb{I} \times \mathbb{I}) \xrightarrow{\sim q} W_{\lambda}. \end{array}$$

The composite $1 \xrightarrow{\delta_0} \mathbb{I} \xrightarrow{m} W_{\lambda}$ is classified by the face lattice and indeed a trivial cofibration, although $m$ is not. By our working assumption, $m\delta_0$ can be written as a cell complex of maps in $S$, in particular as a transfinite composite of maps $i_{\alpha} \colon A_{\alpha} \mapsto A_{\alpha+1}$. Any stage of the transfinite composite that contains the interval $m \colon \mathbb{I} \mapsto W_{\lambda}$ must contain all of $W_{\lambda}$, since we cannot glue on a square along its diagonal.
