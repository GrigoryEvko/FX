*Proof.* We have $\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_i, -) \cong \mathcal{E}(\mathrm{dom}\, u_i, \mathrm{dom}(-)) \times_{\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{dom}\, u_i, \mathrm{cod}(-))} \mathcal{E}(\mathrm{cod}\, u_i, \mathrm{cod}(-))$. From the assumption that $\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{dom}\, u_i, -)$ preserves colimits of $\kappa$-chains in $\mathcal{M}$, it thus follows that $\mathcal{E}^{-}(u_i, -)$ preserves colimits of $\kappa$-chains in $\mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$. For any $\kappa$-chain $f: (\kappa, \preceq) \to \mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$, we then have $\mathrm{colim}_{\alpha < \kappa}(u \downarrow f_\alpha) \cong u \downarrow (\mathrm{colim}_{\alpha < \kappa} f_\alpha)$. The result then follows from the colimit formula for $D_u$ (Definition 3.2.3). $\square$

**Theorem 3.2.12** (Algebraic small object argument with a backdrop). Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a category with a $\kappa$-backdrop $\mathcal{M}$ and let $u: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{E}^{-}$ be a diagram compatible with $\mathcal{M}$ such that $\mathrm{dom}\, u$ is levelwise $(\kappa, \mathcal{M})$-small. Then there is an AWFS $(\mathsf{L}, \mathsf{R})$ cofibrantly generated by $u$.

*Proof.* By Bourke and Garner's result (Proposition 3.2.1), if the algebraically free monad on $\mathsf{Spl}^u_{\mathcal{E}}$ exists, then it is the monad of an AWFS cofibrantly generated by $u$. To construct the free monad on $\mathsf{Spl}^u_{\mathcal{E}}$, it suffices to check that $(\mathcal{E}^{-}, \mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong}), \mathsf{Spl}^u_{\mathcal{E}}) \in \mathrm{ConfMnd}^\kappa_p$:

- 2.3.6(a) $\mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$ is a $\kappa$-backdrop in $\mathcal{E}^{-}$ because $\mathcal{M}$ is a $\kappa$-backdrop in $\mathcal{E}$.
- 2.3.6(b) $\mathsf{spl}^u$ is valued in $\mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$. This follows from 3.2.4(a) by the closure of $\mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$ under cobase change in $\mathcal{E}^{-}$.
- 2.3.6(c) $\mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$ is closed under $\widehat{\mathsf{spl}^u}$. By cocontinuity of Leibniz pushout application in the natural transformation argument [RV14, Lemma 4.8] and the definition of $\mathsf{Spl}^u_{\mathcal{E}}$, pushout application of $\mathsf{spl}^u$ is a cobase change of pushout application of $\mathsf{tgt}\, D_u$. By calculation, pushout application of $\mathsf{tgt}\, D_u$ is isomorphic to $\mathsf{tgt}\, \widehat{D_u}$, which preserves $\mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$ by 3.2.4(a), so the property follows from the closure of $\mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$ under cobase change in $\mathcal{E}^{-}$.
- 2.3.6(d) $\mathsf{Spl}^u_{\mathcal{E}}$ preserves colimits of $\kappa$-chains in $\mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$ by Lemma 3.2.11.

Theorem 2.3.27 thus provides an algebraically free monad $\mathsf{R} = (R, \Lambda, \Pi)$ on $\mathsf{Spl}^u_{\mathcal{E}}$ whose unit is valued in $\mathcal{E}^{-}(\overset{\mathcal{M}}{\cong})$, and by Proposition 3.2.1 we have an AWFS $(\mathsf{L}, \mathsf{R})$. $\square$

**Notation 3.2.13.** In the situation of Theorem 3.2.12, we have a transformation $\mathsf{tgt}\, D_u \to \Lambda$ given by the composite

$$\begin{array}{ccc} D_u & \xrightarrow{\epsilon} & \mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}^{-}} & \xlongequal{\text{Id}_{\mathcal{E}^{-}}} \\ \mathsf{tgt}\, D_u \downarrow & & \downarrow \downarrow \mathsf{spl}^u & \downarrow \downarrow \Lambda \\ \mathrm{Tgt}_{\mathcal{E}}\, D_u & \longrightarrow & \mathrm{Spl}^u_{\mathcal{E}} & \longrightarrow & R. \end{array}$$

We write $\mathrm{step}_u: D_u \to L$ for its whiskering with the domain projection.

### 3.3 Notions of composable structure

At this point we begin working in earnest towards a saturation principle for cofibrantly generated AWFS's. First, we abstract from the situation of $U_{\mathsf{L}_p}: \mathsf{L}_p\text{-Coalg} \to \mathrm{Sp}(\mathcal{E})$ to define a class of *composable notions of structure* on maps in $\mathcal{E}$. We then define what it means for such a notion of structure to be cellular.

The double categories $\mathsf{L}_p$-Coalg and $\mathsf{L}$-Coalg (and their duals $\mathsf{R}_p$-Alg and $\mathsf{R}$-Alg) are what Bourke and Garner call *concrete double categories*, in which the vertical morphisms are horizontal morphisms equipped with some structure. To suit our desired generality, we work with a slightly different abstraction which we call a *notion of composable structure*, imposing some additional sanity conditions but allowing for non-thin and pseudo double categories.$^3$ Permitting non-thin double categories is necessary for our application to constructing extension operations (Section 4.3).

$^3$The name mimics Shulman's *notions of fibred structure* [Shu19, §3]. Both axiomatize notions of structure on the morphisms of a category, but *fibred* structures are closed under base change while *composable* structures can be composed.
