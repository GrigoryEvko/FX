By Theorem 2.3.27, the morphisms of configurations induce corresponding strong morphisms of free monads. The strong morphisms associated to the projections force the free monad on $\overline{\mathsf{T}} \times_{\mathcal{E}} \mathbb{A}^{\natural}$ to be $\overline{\mathsf{M}} \times_{\mathcal{E}} \mathbb{A}^{\natural}$, which makes the strong morphism associated to $(\theta, \star)$ the desired $(\star, \psi): (\mathbb{A}^{\natural} \times_{\mathcal{E}} \mathbb{A}^{\natural}, \overline{\mathsf{M}} \times_{\mathcal{E}} \mathbb{A}^{\natural}) \to (\mathbb{A}^{\natural}, \overline{\mathsf{M}})$. The universal property of $(\overline{\mathsf{M}}, \psi)$ described in the theorem statement follows straightforwardly from the universal property of $\overline{\mathsf{M}}$ as the free monad on $\overline{\mathsf{T}}$. $\square$

Although our target application of Theorem 3.4.4 is to the algebraic small object argument, it also can be used to derive properties of free monads more generally.

Example 3.4.5. Let $\mathcal{E}$ be an elementary topos and let $\mathsf{T} = (T, \tau)$ be a pointed endofunctor on $\mathcal{E}$ that preserves colimits of $\kappa$-chains for some limit ordinal $\kappa > 0$. If $\tau$ is a cartesian natural transformation valued in monomorphisms, then the unit of the free monad on $\mathsf{T}$ (which exists by Theorem 2.3.27) is also cartesian and valued in monomorphisms.

Proof. By Theorem 3.4.4 with $\mathcal{M} := \mathcal{E}$ and the notion of composable structure $U: \mathbb{A} \to \operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{E})$ whose vertical morphisms are monomorphisms in $\mathcal{E}$ and whose squares are pullback squares in $\mathcal{E}$. This notion of composable structure is left-connected, so per Remark 3.4.3 an $\mathbb{A}$-point over $\mathsf{T}$ consists of a lift of $\tau$ through $U^{\natural}$; the assumptions on $\tau$ say exactly that such a lift exists. The existence of a subobject classifier and pullback-stable colimits implies that $\mathbb{A}^{\natural}(\frac{\mathbb{S}}{\mathcal{E}})$ is closed under all colimits. Thus the free monad on $\mathsf{T}$ also admits an $\mathbb{A}$-point, which implies in particular that its unit also lifts through $U^{\natural}$. $\square$

### 3.5 The algebraic small object argument as a saturation
