### 3.2 Configurations for the algebraic small object argument

We now revisit Bourke and Garner's presentation of the algebraic small object argument, which simplifies Garner's original presentation. The construction rests on the following observation:

**Proposition 3.2.1** (Corollary of [BG16a, Theorem 6]). Let $u: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{E}^\to$ be a diagram. If the $u^\pitchfork: \mathcal{J}^\pitchfork \to \mathcal{E}^\to$ is a monadic functor, then its associated monad is, up to isomorphism, the monad of an AWFS cofibrantly generated by $u$.

That is, we can construct an AWFS $u: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{E}^\to$ by exhibiting $u^\pitchfork: \mathcal{J}^\pitchfork \to \mathcal{E}^\to$ as the forgetful functor for a category of algebras for a monad. As a corollary, if $u^\pitchfork: \mathcal{J}^\pitchfork \to \mathcal{E}^\to$ is the forgetful functor for a category of algebras for a *pointed endofunctor*, then we have an AWFS cofibrantly generated by $u$ as soon as we can construct the algebraically free monad on that pointed endofunctor.

Bourke and Garner show that when $\mathcal{E}$ is locally presentable, we can always exhibit such a pointed endofunctor and construct its algebraically free monad by appeal to Kelly [BG16a, Proposition 16]. We instead apply our results from Section 2 assuming a $\kappa$-backdrop in $\mathcal{E}$ compatible with the generating category and a $\kappa$-smallness condition on the domains of the generators.

**Definition 3.2.2.** When $\mathcal{E}$ is a category and $\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{N}$ are wide subcategories of $\mathcal{E}$, write $\mathcal{E}^\to(\frac{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{N}})$ for the wide subcategory of $\mathcal{E}^\to$ consisting of squares $f \to g$ of the form

$$\begin{array}{ccc} A & \xrightarrow{\in \mathcal{M}} & C \\ f \downarrow & & \downarrow g \\ B & \xrightarrow{\in \mathcal{N}} & D. \end{array}$$

**Definition 3.2.3** (Kock [Koc66]). Given a functor $v: \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$, its *density comonad* $D_v: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{D}$ is (when it exists) the pointwise left Kan extension $\operatorname{Lan}_v v: \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{D}$ of $v$ along itself:

$$D_v(d) = \operatorname{colim} \left( v \downarrow d \xrightarrow{\pi} \mathcal{C} \xrightarrow{v} \mathcal{D} \right).$$

**Definition 3.2.4.** Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a category, $\kappa > 0$ be a limit ordinal, and $\mathcal{M}$ be a $\kappa$-backdrop in $\mathcal{E}$. Given a diagram $u: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{E}^\to$, we say $u$ is *compatible with* $\mathcal{M}$ if

- (a) $u$ admits a density comonad $D_u: \mathcal{E}^\to \mathcal{E}^\to$ valued in $\mathcal{M}$;
- (b) the pushout application $\widehat{D_u}: (\mathcal{E}^\to)^\to \mathcal{E}^\to$ sends squares in $\mathcal{E}^\to(\frac{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}})$ to morphisms in $\mathcal{M}$.

Condition 3.2.4(a) implies that the pushout application $\widehat{D_u}$ is defined, by assumption that $\mathcal{M}$ is a backdrop.

**Example 3.2.5.** For any cocomplete category $\mathcal{E}$, diagram $u: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{E}^\to$, and limit ordinal $\kappa > 0$, $u$ is compatible with the $\kappa$-backdrop consisting of all arrows in $\mathcal{E}$.

We use the following proposition to construct uniform fibrations AWFS's in Section 4.1.

**Proposition 3.2.6.** Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a $\kappa$-backdrop in a category $\mathcal{E}$ and suppose $\mathcal{M}$ is closed under binary unions. For $u: \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{E}^\to$ to be compatible with $\mathcal{M}$, it suffices (but is not necessary) that $D_u$ exists, is valued in $\mathcal{M}$, and sends squares in $\mathcal{E}^\to(\frac{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}})$ to cartesian squares in $\mathcal{E}^\to(\frac{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{M}})$.

*Proof.* Under these hypotheses, given a square $(h, k): f \to g$ with $h, k$ in $\mathcal{M}$, $\widehat{D_u}(h, k)$ computes a union of maps in $\mathcal{M}$ and is thus in $\mathcal{M}$ itself.

**Example 3.2.7.** The $\kappa$-backdrop of monomorphisms (Example 2.3.3) in an adhesive and $\kappa$-exhaustive category is closed under binary unions [LS05, Theorem 5.1], so Proposition 3.2.6 can be used in this case. The same goes for the $\omega$-backdrop of complemented monomorphisms (Example 2.3.4) in a category with coproducts [GSS22, Lemma 2.1.7(i)], and likewise for the $\omega$-backdrop of levelwise complemented monomorphisms in a functor category $[\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{E}]$ where $\mathcal{E}$ has coproducts.
