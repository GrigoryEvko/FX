- $A \in \mathcal{E}$ is $(\kappa, \mathcal{M})$-small if $\mathcal{E}(A, -)$ preserves colimits of $\kappa$-chains in $\mathcal{M}$ (Definition 3.2.8);
- $u$ is compatible with $\mathcal{M}$ if $D_u \colon \mathcal{E}^\to \mathcal{E}^\to$ is valued in $\mathcal{M}$ and its pushout application $\widehat{D_u} \colon (\mathcal{E}^\to)^\to \mathcal{E}^\to$ sends squares levelwise in $\mathcal{M}$ to morphisms in $\mathcal{M}$ (Definition 3.2.4);
- $U \colon \mathbb{A} \to \operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{E})$ is $(\kappa, \mathcal{M})$-cellular if the wide subcategory $\mathbb{A}^\sharp(\frac{\cong}{\mathcal{M}}) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{A}^\sharp$ of squares with upper horizontal map an isomorphism and lower horizontal map in $\mathcal{M}$ is closed under cobase change in $\mathbb{A}^\sharp$, and $(1 + \alpha)$-chains in $\mathbb{A}^\sharp(\frac{\cong}{\mathcal{M}})$ have colimits in $\mathbb{A}^\sharp$ for $\alpha \preceq \kappa$ (Definition 3.3.7).

If $\mathcal{E}$ is cocomplete, one can always take $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{E}$, but choosing a smaller backdrop gives a stronger result; often we take $\mathcal{M}$ to be a class of monomorphisms.

Our second main theorem, derived from the first, shows that if $U \colon \mathbb{A} \to \operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{E})$ also functorially lifts codomain retracts (Definition 3.5.10) then we can assign an $\mathbb{A}$-structure to every left map:

**Theorem 3.5.13.** Fix a category $\mathcal{E}$ equipped with a $\kappa$-backdrop $\mathcal{M}$, a diagram $u \colon \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{E}^\to$ compatible with $\mathcal{M}$ such that $\operatorname{dom} u$ is levelwise $(\kappa, \mathcal{M})$-small, and a left-connected, $(\kappa, \mathcal{M})$-cellular notion of composable structure $U \colon \mathbb{A} \to \operatorname{Sq}(\mathcal{E})$ with a codomain retract lifting operator. If $(\mathsf{L}, \mathsf{R})$ is the AWFS cofibrantly generated by $u$, then any lift $D_\mathbb{A} \colon \mathcal{E}^\to \mathbb{A}^\sharp$ of $D_u \colon \mathcal{E}^\to \mathcal{E}^\to$ through $U^\sharp$ induces a functor $j \colon \mathsf{L}_p\text{-Coalg} \to \mathbb{A}^\sharp$ fitting in the diagram

$$\begin{array}{c} \mathsf{L}_p\text{-Coalg} \xrightarrow{\quad j \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad U_{\mathsf{L}_p} \\ \searrow \quad \searrow \quad \searrow \quad \swarrow U^\sharp \end{array}$$

If the retract lifting operator interacts suitably with composition of left maps, we can even upgrade $j$ to a pseudo double functor from the double category of left maps to $\mathbb{A}$ (Theorem 3.5.16), although we prove this only in the case that $\mathbb{A}$ is a *thin* pseudo double category.

Implicit in the above is the existence of the cofibrantly generated AWFS under the given hypotheses, which we prove first:

**Theorem 3.2.12.** Let $\mathcal{E}$ be a category with a $\kappa$-backdrop $\mathcal{M}$ and let $u \colon \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{E}^\to$ be a diagram compatible with $\mathcal{M}$ such that $\operatorname{dom} u$ is levelwise $(\kappa, \mathcal{M})$-small. Then there is an AWFS $(\mathsf{L}, \mathsf{R})$ cofibrantly generated by $u$.

To prove Theorem 3.2.12, we use Bourke and Garner's proof [BG16a, Theorem 6] that to cofibrantly generate an AWFS $(\mathsf{L}, \mathsf{R})$ on a diagram $u$, it suffices to show that a certain pointed endofunctor on $\mathcal{E}^\to$ (Definition 3.2.9) admits a free and algebraically free monad, which then becomes the monad $\mathsf{R}$ of the AWFS. We instantiate this result with a free monad construction due to Kelly [Kel80, §5 and §14]. In Section 2 we analyze the functoriality of this construction, formulating it as a functor from a category $\operatorname{ConfMnd}_p^\kappa$ of configurations (Definition 2.3.6), whose objects $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{M}, \mathsf{T}) \in \operatorname{ConfMnd}_p^\kappa$ are suitable pointed endofunctors $\mathsf{T} = (T, \tau)$ on a category $\mathcal{E}$ equipped with a $\kappa$-backdrop $\mathcal{M}$, to a category $\mathbf{Mnd}_s$ whose objects $(\mathcal{E}, \mathsf{M}) \in \mathbf{Mnd}_s$ are monads $\mathsf{M}$ on a category $\mathcal{E}$:

**Theorem 2.3.27.** The projection $\operatorname{ConfMnd}_p^\kappa \to \mathbf{Cat}$ lifts to a functor $\operatorname{ConfMnd}_p^\kappa \to \mathbf{Mnd}_s$ sending $(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{M}, \mathsf{T})$ to the free and algebraically free monad on $\mathsf{T}$.

We do not assume $\mathcal{E}$ is cocomplete, showing that the construction only requires the colimits provided by the $\kappa$-backdrop $\mathcal{M}$ under the assumptions that $\tau$ is valued in $\mathcal{M}$ and that its pushout application $\widehat{\tau}$ preserves $\mathcal{M}$. The functoriality in Theorem 2.3.27 tells us that any strong morphism between pointed endofunctors preserving these colimits induces a strong morphism between their free monads. This observation comes from previously circulated but unpublished work of the second author [Sat25]. This functoriality is key to our proof of Theorem 3.5.6.
